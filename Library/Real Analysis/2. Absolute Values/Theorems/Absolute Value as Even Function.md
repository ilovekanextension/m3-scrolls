**Theorem.** Let $a\in \mathbb{R}$. Then, $|a|=|{-a}|$.

**Proof.** By [[Positivity of Squares of Real Numbers]], $1\geq 0$, so $|1|=1$. Also, by [[Orders Between Additive and Multiplicative Inverses]] and [[Inverses of Group Identities]], since $1\geq 0$ we have $-1\leq 0$, so $|{-1}|=-(-1)=1$. Now observe that, by [[Multiplications with Additive Inverses of Ring Ones]] and [[Absolute Value as Norm on Real Numbers]],
$$\begin{align}
|{-a}| & =|(-1)\cdot a| \\
 & =|{-1}|\cdot|a| \\
 & =1\cdot|a| \\
 & =|a|.\blacksquare
\end{align}$$
